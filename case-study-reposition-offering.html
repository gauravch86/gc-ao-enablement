Reposition an underperforming services offering
in the portfolio

A world-class telecom vendor holds a named services offering — Application Managed Services — with strong technical delivery and customer satisfaction on live accounts, but flat net sales and declining gross margin over the last two years. Global Sales cannot articulate differentiated value versus systems-integrator competitors; pricing is inconsistent across regions; Deal Desk approvals are slow on complex bids. Leadership asks: evolve, reposition, or sunset — and wants a plan.

Executive framing: This is a commercial and go-to-market problem, not a delivery turnaround. The offering is sold as headcount and effort hours while buyers purchase business outcomes, cost predictability, and risk reduction. The job: decide the portfolio fate, then fix packaging, pricing discipline, Sales enablement, and investment sequence.

Thought process for interviewers: Start with evidence, not intuition. Separate delivery quality from commercial failure modes. Choose evolve / reposition / sunset with explicit trade-offs. If repositioning: change what Sales sells and how Deal Desk prices — before funding more tooling.

Deliverable 1 · Diagnosis framework

What data to gather — five lenses before any portfolio call

Run a four-week commercial diagnostic (parallel workstreams, one steering forum) before recommending evolve / reposition / sunset. Each lens has an owner, a data source, and a decision trigger.

Lens A

Win / loss and pipeline quality

  • Inputs: 24-month customer-relationship-management pipeline; stage conversion; bid / no-bid reasons; competitive set per deal
  • Interviews: 15–20 won and lost deals — buyer, procurement, technical evaluator
  • Segment by: region, deal size, greenfield versus renewal, application versus infrastructure scope
  • Trigger: if “price only” >40% of losses → packaging/pricing broken; if “value unclear” >30% → positioning broken
Lens B

Margin and deal economics

  • Inputs: profit-and-loss by account and offering; gross-margin trend; transition / knowledge-transfer actuals versus bid
  • Discount waterfall: list price → approved price → signed price; regional variance; Deal Desk override rate
  • Scope creep: change-request volume; unpaid extras; contracted versus actual staffing
  • Trigger: if most margin erosion is discount (not delivery) → commercial guardrails gap
Lens C

Competitive and market shift

  • Inputs: analyst views; competitor rate cards; how peers package “outcome” versus “staff augmentation”
  • Map: who wins on labour arbitrage versus who wins on outcome commitments and commercial clarity
  • Attach: whether this offering pulls through vendor products / adjacent services — or stands alone as a commodity
  • Trigger: if buyers ask for outcome tiers and we still quote pure headcount → reposition required
Lens D

Customer and field voice

  • Inputs: Net Promoter Score / customer satisfaction by account; quarterly business review themes; escalation logs
  • Sales survey: “What do you wish you could sell — and what do you avoid proposing?”
  • Delivery: what customers praise versus what they refuse to pay a premium for
  • Trigger: high delivery scores + low willingness to renew at rate → value-proposition mismatch
Lens E · Portfolio fit

Strategic adjacency and sunset criteria

  • Inputs: overlap with adjacent portfolio offers; cannibalization risk; sunk tooling cost; Finance revenue-recognition constraints
  • Sunset test: no credible path to target gross margin within 18 months and no strategic pull-through → sunset candidate
  • Reposition test: strong delivery + fixable commercial model + Sales demand for a clearer story → reposition candidate
  • Evolve-only test: tooling or process refresh without changing the sellable unit → only if packaging and pricing are already healthy

Diagnostic scorecard (how the call is synthesized)

Signal Healthy Amber Red → action
Pipeline conversion (qualified → won) >25% 15–25% <15% → fix value proposition and Sales enablement

Diagnosis · Expected synthesis

Root causes — commercial, not operational

Based on the scenario inputs, expect three reinforcing commercial root causes. None of them require a delivery turnaround as the primary move.

Root cause 1

Commoditized packaging

  • Offer is sold as staffing towers (Level 1 / Level 2 / Level 3 support) with weak link to business outcomes
  • Sales defaults to rate-per-person — easy for procurement to bench against systems integrators
  • Little modular entry — buyer feels they must purchase the full pyramid or nothing
  • Symptom: flat sales; long cycles; technical wins that die in procurement
Root cause 2

Pricing inconsistency and margin leakage

  • Regions discount independently to hit quota — no enforceable global floor
  • Transition and knowledge-transfer underestimated in the bid → Year-1 margin bleed
  • Scope creep absorbed to protect satisfaction — unpaid work erodes gross margin
  • Symptom: gross margin down 4–6 points while delivery scores hold
Root cause 3

Value story Sales cannot tell

  • Buyers ask for predictable cost, service levels, and risk ownership
  • Field pitch remains headcount replacement and tool-agnostic runbooks
  • No standard one-pager, return-on-investment model, or Deal Desk toolkit that travels globally
  • Symptom: “Sales cannot explain the value”; losses on narrative, not capability
Implication: Sunset would discard a delivery-capable franchise and account footprint. Evolve-only (more tooling, same sellable unit) will not fix flat sales or pricing chaos. The play is reposition the commercial offer, then evolve selected modules underneath where they improve margin or win rate.
Deliverable 2 · Recommendation and trade-offs

Reposition — with selective evolve; do not sunset

Recommendation: REPOSITION Application Managed Services from “headcount-based application support” to Outcome-Tiered Application Operations — a clear commercial ladder Sales can sell, Deal Desk can price, and Finance can recognize.

Sunset

Discards delivery DNA and installed base. High exit cost; competitors inherit footprint. Use only if the 18-month margin path fails the Lens E test — not indicated here.

Evolve only

Tooling or process refresh without changing the sellable unit. Improves delivery incrementally but does not fix Sales narrative or regional pricing chaos. Necessary later; insufficient alone.

Reposition ✓

Change what we sell (outcome tiers + modular packs), how we price (global rate card + floors), and how Sales tells the story. Evolve modules only where they raise win rate or margin.

What reposition means

From → To

  • From: “We provide X people for application support”
  • To: “We commit to availability and restore-time service classes — with a modular path to higher performance and efficiency”
  • Three commercial tiers: Stabilize · Optimize · Transform
  • Staffing remains an internal delivery plan — not the customer-facing product
Trade-offs accepted

What we give up / risks

  • Near-term average deal size: tier entry may be smaller than a full staffing pyramid — offset by attach and renewal step-up
  • Sales productivity dip: one to two quarters while enablement lands
  • Upfront product / go-to-market cost: rate card, service-level catalogue, Deal Desk toolkit
  • Risk of inaction: continued margin bleed and a commodity label that becomes permanent
Updated value proposition (one line): “Ericsson Application Operations — outcome-tiered managed applications that restore control, improve total cost of ownership where contracted, and give Sales a globally consistent story with Deal Desk pricing discipline.”
Deliverable 3 · Pricing, packaging and value proposition

Revised commercial model — tiers, not staffing towers

Packaging shifts from headcount blocks to outcome tiers plus modular capability packs. The customer buys a service class and committed performance; delivery sizing stays internal.

Tier Customer outcome What is included Pricing model Typical buyer
Stabilize Restore control — backlog reduction, service-level baseline, single accountability Application operations coverage · information-technology service management integration · monthly performance board · top-offender (highest-impact defect) program Platform fee + unit price by application criticality · floor gross margin 22% Chief Information Officer / Head of Applications — “stop the bleeding”
Optimize Measurable efficiency — lower run cost and faster mean time to restore where contracted Stabilize + standard monitoring / correlation pack · runbook automation · proactive problem management Outcome-band pricing against agreed service levels · optional gain-share on proven cost reduction Chief Operating Officer / Vice President of Operations — “prove value before broader change”
Transform Operating-model uplift — higher automation share, fewer fire drills, clearer executive scorecard Optimize + continuous-improvement factory · policy-based standard changes · executive scorecard and milestone reviews Milestone-linked commercial elements with Finance revenue-recognition sign-off before proposal Chief Technology Officer / Chief Digital Officer — “change how work is run”

Modular attach packs (priced)

  • Transition / knowledge-transfer module — mandatory on new logos; stops Year-1 margin bleed
  • Top-offender sprint — time-boxed backlog reduction program
  • Monitoring jump-start — 90-day standard pack (not a custom build)
  • Multi-vendor governance option — only when win/loss shows it; optional, not the core thesis

Pricing mechanics

  • Global rate card — list price by tier × application criticality class
  • Regional index — ±15% band (not ad-hoc ±40%)
  • Transition — fixed-fee knowledge-transfer module on every new logo
  • Renewal step-up — tier upgrade clause when performance gates are met

What Sales can finally say

  • “Start with Stabilize — not a multi-year staffing gamble”
  • “Service levels and accountability are contractual”
  • “One commercial language globally — Deal Desk can approve fast”
  • “Upsell Optimize / Transform when value is proven”

When multi-vendor consolidation applies: If win/loss shows buyers are consolidating suppliers to cut governance cost, offer a priced optional pack (single accountability, fewer interfaces) — not as the default reposition story. Do not require consolidation to justify the tier model.

Deliverable 3 (continued) · Commercial guardrails and Deal Desk

Pricing guidelines for Sales and Deal Desk on complex deals

Guardrails turn regional chaos into predictable margin. Deal Desk becomes a deal architect — trading scope, term, and tier — not a discount rubber stamp.

Guardrail Rule Deal Desk action if breached
Floor gross margin Stabilize ≥22% · Optimize ≥26% · Transform ≥28% (after transition) Escalate to Regional Product Head; require scope reduction or tier downgrade
Maximum discount ≤12% off list without executive approval; ≤8% on renewals Discount must be traded for longer term, reduced scope, or contracted tier step-up
Transition pricing Knowledge-transfer module mandatory on all new logos; minimum 8% of Year-1 annual contract value No exceptions — underpriced transition is the leading margin killer in the diagnostic
Scope change Out-of-scope change request within five business days; unpaid work capped at 2% of annual contract value Service Delivery Manager and Product Owner sign-off; feeds account profit-and-loss review
Regional variance Like-for-like bids within ±15% of the global rate-card index Above 15% requires written competitive intel and win-strategy approval
Service credits / penalties Soft-ramp months 1–6; credit caps by tier; Finance confirms revenue-recognition treatment before signature No customer proposal with milestone or credit elements until Finance signs off

Deal Desk toolkit

  • Return-on-investment calculator — customer-facing cost and risk narrative
  • Margin waterfall — internal bid model (labour, tools, transition, risk)
  • Tier selector — discovery questionnaire → recommended sellable unit
  • Competitive battlecards — versus staff-augmentation integrators and cloud managed-service providers
  • Executive one-pager per tier — what the buyer gets in 90 days

Complex-deal playbook

  • Multi-tower bids: land Application Operations first; expand adjacent towers with separate statements of work under one governance
  • Renewal at risk: tier step-up or modular attach — never a pure rate cut as the strategy
  • Partner-heavy estates: price adapters and accountability clearly; do not hide margin risk in “we’ll sort it later”
  • Consolidation deals (when applicable): optional pack with explicit customer governance savings — not a free giveaway
Deliverable 4 · Investment prioritization

Sequence for return on investment — fund, defer, stop

Repositioning fails if everything is funded at once. Sequence commercial assets first (they unblock Sales this quarter), then repeatable delivery standards, and explicitly stop low-return work.

Quarter 1 · 0–90 days
Sell differently

Global rate card · tier definitions · return-on-investment calculator · battlecards · Deal Desk rules live

Quarter 2 · 3–6 months
Deliver to tier

Standard service-level catalogue · transition factory · two regional pilots

Quarters 3–4 · 6–12 months
Attach and renew

Modular packs · renewal step-up motions · scale enablement beyond pilots

Year 2
Portfolio harvest

Retire legacy headcount-only contracts on renewal · expand Transform where demand is proven

Fund (high return)

Priority investments

  • Global rate card and margin model — stops bleeding immediately
  • Standard service-level / performance catalogue — one language for Sales and Delivery
  • Sales and Deal Desk enablement — tier pitch, discovery kit, executive one-pagers
  • Priced transition module — protects Year-1 gross margin on every new logo
  • Two regional pilots — prove reposition before a global mandate
Deprioritize / stop

Explicitly not now

  • Per-account custom tooling builds — use standard packs; charge for exceptions
  • Below-floor proofs of concept sold to “win a logo”
  • Regional bespoke offerings that break the global rate card
  • Marketing rebrand without Sales / Deal Desk kit — story with no conversion path
  • Large platform research and development before commercial motion is fixed
Return-on-investment logic: Quarter-1 spend is mostly product and go-to-market labour — low capital expense, immediate guardrail effect. Quarters 2–4 standardize delivery so margin holds when volume returns. Year 2 expands only what pilots proved — not a pre-committed technology roadmap.
Deliverable 5 · Cross-functional alignment

Who does what — Sales, Marketing, Finance, regions

Repositioning is a portfolio program, not a slide deck. Each function has a named outcome, artifact, and cadence.

Sales and Bid / Deal Desk

  • Outcome: ≥80% of new Application Managed Services bids use the tier sellable unit and rate card by Quarter 2
  • Artifacts: discovery kit · return-on-investment calculator · margin waterfall · approval matrix
  • Cadence: weekly deal review on pilot opportunities; win/loss tagged to tier
  • Lead: Global Sales Enablement with Regional Product Manager (matrix)

Marketing and Portfolio

  • Outcome: one external narrative — “Application Operations by outcome tier”
  • Artifacts: tier one-pagers · proof stories · analyst brief; retire “staffing tower” language from collateral
  • Cadence: monthly message test with Sales
  • Lead: Services Portfolio Marketing with Strategic Product Manager ownership

Finance — profit and loss, revenue recognition, compliance

  • Outcome: tier pricing compliant with revenue recognition; gross-margin floors in the enterprise resource planning bid model
  • Artifacts: revenue-recognition memo per tier (platform fee versus milestone elements) · credit/penalty accounting
  • Cadence: Deal Desk sign-off on any outcome or milestone element before customer proposal
  • Lead: Regional Finance Business Partner with Global Services Controller

Regional enablement and Delivery

  • Outcome: pilot regions deliver to the standard service-level catalogue; transition factory is repeatable
  • Artifacts: knowledge-transfer playbook · performance board for Service Delivery Managers · escalation path to Product
  • Cadence: bi-weekly pilot steering; quarterly business reviews propose tier step-ups on live accounts
  • Lead: Regional Delivery Head with Product Owner for the offer

Program governance

  • Steering forum: Strategic Product Manager (chair) · Sales Vice President · Marketing · Finance · two Regional Managing Directors — monthly
  • Decision rights: Product owns sellable unit and tier; Finance owns revenue recognition; Sales owns pipeline targets; Delivery owns service-level attainment
  • Change management: train-the-trainer in pilot regions; audit “shadow rate cards” through Deal Desk
Deliverable 6 · Success metrics and feedback loop

How we know reposition worked — and how we keep refining

Split leading indicators (commercial motion) from lagging indicators (profit-and-loss outcomes). A quarterly Pricing Council adjusts tiers, floors, and packs from field data — not an annual slide refresh.

Net sales growth (Application Managed Services) Flat (~0%) +8–12% (tier land + attach) Sales Vice President
Gross margin % −4–6 points versus two years ago Recover 3–4 points; within 2 points of portfolio target Finance / Strategic Product Manager

Regional price variance (like-for-like) >25% <15% Strategic Product Manager
Wins citing value / total-cost story <20% ≥40% of wins Marketing / Sales
Tier step-up / attach at renewal Not applicable (no tiers) ≥25% of renewals upsell Optimize or Transform Regional Delivery

Quarterly Pricing Council

  • Review win/loss tagged by tier, discount depth, competitor
  • Adjust rate-card index at most ±3% per cycle
  • Add or retire modular packs based on attach data
  • Escalate systemic delivery gaps to Product / Delivery leadership

Monthly field signals

  • Deal Desk override log → guardrail patches
  • Change-request volume per account → packaging fix
  • Sales survey: “What blocked the bid?”
  • Pilot service-level attainment → tier credibility proof

Annual portfolio review

  • Re-run the sunset test — gross-margin trajectory versus 18-month rule
  • Retire legacy headcount-only contracts on renewal
  • Confirm Transform demand before further product investment
  • Refresh competitive battlecards and analyst narrative

Transferable commercial judgment

How prior deal work informs this call

  • Large managed-services renewals: when the commercial story breaks, transparency and accountability restore trust faster than a rate cut
  • Complex bids: priced transition, clear service levels, and discount-versus-margin trade-offs protect both customer value and vendor economics
  • Those patterns transfer to portfolio packaging — even when the offering itself is a new commercial problem
