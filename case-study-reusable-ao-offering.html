Reusable Autonomous Operations offering
anchored on customer-agreed KPIs / OKRs

A large telecom operator running a multi-vendor, multi-domain network under margin and reliability pressure. Today: reactive operations, high incident volumes, manual troubleshooting, inconsistent service-level agreements across regions. Ericsson proposes a managed services offering that evolves the customer from reactive → predictive → autonomous over a defined horizon — reusable across customers, anchored on measurable autonomy-centric key performance indicators and objectives and key results.

North star: End-user experience and Net Promoter Score · service-level / contractual performance-indicator attainment · market trust — with customer total cost of ownership and a reusable commercial offering as how we fund and scale that outcome.
Base assumption (given): The solution at this customer includes Ericsson products and third-party portfolio under the Managed Services umbrella — we productize operations across that mixed estate, not an Ericsson-only silo.
Autonomy levels · KPI / OKR contract Design → Deploy → Operate → Assure → Improve Reuse · enablement · outcome commercial Assumptions → pilots before scale

Credibility lens (lived): Patterns proven in Telefónica Argentina (top-offender discipline → renewal) and Three UK (vendor consolidation, single pane of glass, self-healing, 474→336 full-time-equivalent economics, lowest Priority-1 incidents).

Executive glossary (first use): KPI = key performance indicator · OKR = objectives and key results · TCO = total cost of ownership · SLA = service-level agreement · CPI = contractual performance indicator · MTTR = mean time to restore · FTE = full-time equivalent · SPOG = single pane of glass · 3PP = third-party products · HITL = human-in-the-loop · SDM = Service Delivery Manager · P&L = profit and loss.

Step 1 · Economic unlock (enabler, not the end goal)

TCO reduction via vendor consolidation

Intra- and inter-team inertia across vendors drives poor MTTR, noisy KPIs, and a large customer governance tax (SLA chasing, vendor mgmt, ITIL overhead). Consolidation is the operating-model unlock that makes single accountability, one observability plane, and AO possible — with customer TCO and −20% governance as the economic proof, not the offering’s north star.

Step 2 · Integrated observability & self-healing

One health view · then AI-act on anomalies

Target: a fully integrated observability plane so one incident’s dependencies across Infra, Cloud, Application and Network are correlated fast — then plug in self-healing + anomaly detection (AI/ML) on a reusable metrics template library.

1 · Unified observability

  • Single pane for landscape health
  • Cross-domain correlation (Infra ↔ Cloud ↔ App ↔ Network)
  • Faster triage, better reaction time, lower MTTR
  • SDM sees business impact, not only infra noise

2 · Self-healing + anomaly AI

  • Detect anomaly → classify → act → verify
  • Exhaustive metrics templates per domain
  • Drag-drop library per account (reuse)
  • SOPs versioned; feed continuous learning

3 · Tool consolidation path

  • Log-mining + API orchestration for bug/integration trace
  • Link incident ↔ data/integration path
  • Enable sunset of workarounds / self-heal scripts once RC lands
  • Backwards-compatible AI-ready stack

Step 3 · Data-driven sizing & SLA CPI / OKR design

6–12 month incident dump → maturity → risk-safe targets

Experts + AI/ML enrichment turn raw incidents into a clear maturity view per App / IT / Network component. Contractual CPIs (availability, response/restore MTTR, problem aging, change success, SR SLAs) plus commercial OKRs (TCO / governance) are sized for Reactive → Predictive → Autonomous with soft-ramp windows that avoid penalty cliffs.

Discovery method

  • Ingest 6–12 months incident dump
  • SME review + AI/ML for correctness & context enrichment
  • Classify maturity per component
  • Build top-offender backlog (volume × business impact)
  • Seed reusable metrics / SOP library from day one

Value of single leadership on CPIs

  • L1→L3→Deploy→SDM Incident→Problem/sunset under one owner
  • No finger-pointing; digital CPI board watched daily by SDM (Avail 20% credit, MTTR 10%, …)
  • Accountability boosts NPS / partner trust / end-user experience
  • Surfaced deep bugs/integrations become new revenue streams

Risk-safe ramp (avoid penalty cliff)

  • Response CPIs allow 90% in first 6 months post commencement — align Discovery→KT→Shadow to this window
  • Milestone P&L gates (M3–M18) unlock autonomy claims only after credit-weighted CPI trends stabilize
  • Digital SDM board: daily watch on Availability (20% credit), P0/P1 restore (10%), P2 restore (10%), Change success (10%), Final RCA (10%), …

How these CPIs prioritize the AO roadmap

  • Rank automation backlog by credit weight × breach risk × volume
  • First: Availability + P0/P1 MTTR ≤4h → then repeat-incident ≤10% & Known Error schedule ≥60%
  • Change success 98% / emergency ≤5% = proof of predictive maturity
  • Ticket quality ≥95% = prerequisite before expanding AI autonomy

Lived practice: Large-account MGS used a full CPI/KPI annex (availability tiers, incident MTTR, problem aging, change success, SR SLAs) — ~75 operational measures from transition. TEF Argentina used offender + CxO KPI transparency to unlock renewal. Catalogue above is anonymized from that SLA style — not a reproduction of any confidential annex.

Step 4 · Architecture for reuse

Productized Operations · drag-drop capability library

Not a bespoke project. A global reusable library of monitoring metrics, self-heal packs and SOPs — so success in one account can be recreated worldwide. Multi-vendor opacity hid problems; single accountability surfaces them — and creates upsell / modernization revenue.

Reusable product core

  • Autonomy maturity framework & phase gates
  • Exhaustive metrics templates (Cloud / Infra / App / Network)
  • Self-heal + anomaly packs (versioned)
  • SPOG correlation patterns
  • CPI scorecard (Avail / MTTR / Problem / Change) & SDM board
  • GTM tiers, pricing guardrails, enablement kit

Per-account configure (not fork)

  • Drag-drop metrics from standard library
  • Intent / policy thresholds
  • Connector mapping to estate tools
  • Localized AI use-cases
  • Org model (L1–L3, A-team, SDM)

Trade-offs (state openly)

  • More standardization up front → faster Nth logo
  • Deep custom = margin & reuse risk
  • Start human-in-loop; earn full autonomy
  • Thin adapters > tool lock-in
  • Surfaced defects → new paid streams (feature, not failure)
Portfolio narrative: end-to-end integrated operations product under the MS umbrella (Ericsson + 3PP) — replace fragmented vendors with a coherent path to autonomous operations, backed by reusable IP, not a shallow AO story.
Deliverable · Adoption & enablement

Enable customer ops · enable Ericsson regions

Reuse fails without adoption. Plan covers training, phased rollout, resistance management, and success criteria for both the customer’s operations teams and Ericsson’s regions.

Customer operations teams

Commercial depth · 5-year MGS P&L construction

P&L verticals · FTE ramp + site-mix (envelope with gravity)

Minimum 5-year (60-month) MGS deal. Baseline multi-vendor headcount = B. Ericsson stand-up target X = B − 30%. Customer is committed a 20% run-cost reduction; we size at 30% — the 10-point gap is structural EBIT, not a sales promise.

① FTE ramp (mean headcount path → 30% below B over 60 months)

Year Mean FTE vs target X Rationale
Y1 X + 20% 1.20X Discovery / KT / shadow peak — protect CPI soft-ramp
Y2 X + 10% 1.10X HITL packs live; predictive coverage expanding
Y3 X 1.00X Steady-state target operating model
Y4 X − 10% 0.90X Autonomy + reuse harvest
Y5 X − 20% 0.80X Closed-loop scale; library compound

5-year mean = (1.2+1.1+1.0+0.9+0.8)/5 × X = X = 0.7B → run-cost ≈ 30% below B×60. Customer price narrative commits −20% vs baseline → structural labor EBIT ≈ +10%.

② Site-mix

Onsite leadership · offshore / near-shore bulk

  • Onsite: account leadership + technical module leads per domain — shoulder-to-shoulder with customer focals
  • Capture depth/breadth in Discovery; own tooling consolidation & business-value delivery
  • Man-to-man mapping with customer domain focals → kill noise before manager escalation
  • Offshore / near-shore: remainder of X — delivery factories for L1–L3, automation, Problem/KE factories
  • Site-mix adds ≈ +10% EBIT → headline 20%; park 5% into penalty envelope → operating view 15%
EBIT bridge (so far)

From labor math to envelope

  • FTE sizing gap (30% size / 20% commit) → +10%
  • Site-mix leverage → +10% (cumulative 20%)
  • Park to penalty lot → −5% (operating 15%)
  • Illustrative: if B = 100 FTE, X = 70; Y1–Y5 = 84 / 77 / 70 / 63 / 56
Commercial depth · Tools · licenses · penalty envelope

P&L verticals · reusable IP, license recovery, penalty parking lot

Finish the bridge so the proposition has commercial gravity — reusable product investment, customer license re-investment, and an explicit SLA/penalty envelope (not a surprise clause).

③ Tools & product R&D

Observability · self-heal · AI SOP · HITL

  • Reusable frameworks are Portfolio / Product R&D assets — not a full dump on one account
  • A portion maps to this P&L; work starts immediately (cannot wait on bureaucracy)
  • Versioned packs + continuous learning feed into Ericsson products under MS
  • Initial investment ≈ −5% EBIT → operating view back to 10% while tools commence
④ Licensing (smart recovery)

Customer re-invests · we recover Y1 tool seed

  • Estimate current customer license/infra tooling spend (reuse or replace with AO stack)
  • Customer budgets optimized modern licenses — no net new wallet if spend is redirected
  • That re-investment covers ongoing licenses and recovers the ≈5% seed from ③ over Year-1
  • After Y1 recovery → EBIT back to 15%; transition team owns quality gates so reinvestment lands on time
⑤ Penalty parking lot

Envelope parameters · not a surprise

  • The 5% parked at site-mix is the commercial envelope for SLA/credit exposure
  • Contract: recovery / earn-back mechanisms; soft-ramp; credit caps aligned to CPI annex
  • If execution fails a gate → envelope absorbs; if gates & SLAs are met → parked 5% converts to additional EBIT
  • No end-of-year “gotcha” — penalty is a planned P&L line, not a shock
